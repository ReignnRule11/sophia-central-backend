# Sophis Central — Monetization and 12-Month Growth Roadmap

**Status:** Proposed sequence for validation; dates and numeric targets must be approved after baseline measurement.

## Guiding principles

- Sell measurable institutional outcomes, not an unbounded feature list.
- Validate willingness to pay before investing in broad monetization infrastructure.
- Keep essential academic access and governance fair and accessible.
- Do not charge for features that are not implemented, supportable and production-verified.
- Tie hiring and infrastructure upgrades to demand, workload and service-level evidence.

## Stage 0 — Establish the baseline

**Objective:** Know what exists, who uses it, what it costs and what remains unverified.

Work:
- Confirm production architecture, provider accounts, regions and service owners.
- Collect current invoices, subscriptions, usage and renewal dates.
- Verify key student, lecturer, reviewer, moderation and admin workflows.
- Establish active-user and institution-level product analytics with privacy safeguards.
- Define support, incident and data-governance responsibilities.
- Complete the technical scaling assessment and populate the financial model.

Exit evidence:
- Approved service inventory and actual monthly cost baseline.
- Production workflow verification report.
- Instrumented product and reliability metrics.
- Named owners for security, operations and customer support.

## Stage 1 — Validate the institutional offer

**Objective:** Determine who buys, what problem they fund and what procurement requires.

Work:
- Interview institutional decision-makers, lecturers, administrators and students.
- Identify budget owners, procurement cycles, deployment requirements and data policies.
- Test a small number of clearly differentiated subscription packages.
- Define a paid pilot scope with success criteria, timeline, responsibilities and exit terms.
- Prepare onboarding, training, support and data-handling documentation.

Metrics:
- Qualified institutional opportunities.
- Discovery-to-pilot conversion.
- Pilot activation and workflow completion.
- Time to onboard an institution.
- User satisfaction and support burden.
- Willingness to pay and renewal intent.

Exit evidence:
- Documented buyer feedback.
- Validated pricing hypotheses.
- Pilot agreements or clearly recorded pipeline stages.
- Delivery cost and implementation effort measured.

## Stage 2 — Convert pilots into repeatable revenue

**Objective:** Prove that the offer can generate recurring revenue and be delivered consistently.

Work:
- Convert successful pilots to annual or otherwise recurring agreements.
- Standardize institution onboarding and training.
- Establish account ownership, support escalation and renewal calendar.
- Measure infrastructure and support cost per institution.
- Review product adoption and retention by cohort.
- Improve the most-used workflows before adding speculative features.

Metrics:
- Paying institutions and contracted recurring revenue.
- Cash collection and receivables aging.
- Pilot-to-paid conversion.
- Renewal/retention and expansion.
- Gross margin and cost to serve.
- Onboarding time and support tickets per institution.

Exit evidence:
- Paid contracts and collected revenue.
- Documented renewal/retention evidence.
- Repeatable implementation playbook.
- Positive contribution economics or a credible path to them.

## Stage 3 — Expand responsibly

**Objective:** Grow distribution without compromising platform reliability, security or support.

Work:
- Expand to adjacent institutions or departments using validated channels.
- Add customer success/implementation capacity when workload requires it.
- Increase cloud/database/storage capacity only at measured thresholds.
- Formalize security review, backup recovery and incident response.
- Evaluate integrations based on signed customer needs and lifecycle costs.
- Introduce optional premium features only after product and cost validation.

Metrics:
- Net revenue retention where applicable.
- New institution acquisition and sales-cycle duration.
- Customer acquisition cost and payback, once enough cohort data exists.
- Gross margin and infrastructure cost per active institution.
- Availability, error rate and incident resolution.
- Support response and onboarding capacity.

## Monetization experiments

| Experiment | Hypothesis | Evidence required before launch |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional annual subscription | Institutions will pay for governed academic resource and workflow capabilities | Buyer interviews, pilot demand, cost-to-serve and procurement feedback |
| Department/faculty package | Smaller buyers need a lower-commitment entry point | Department-level budget authority and willingness-to-pay |
| Paid onboarding/training | Institutions value assisted adoption and configuration | Delivery effort, scope, repeatability and customer acceptance |
| Enhanced support/analytics | Larger institutions need higher service and reporting levels | Implemented capability, usage evidence and support cost |
| Optional student/lecturer upgrades | Some users will pay for incremental convenience or productivity | User research, fairness review, conversion test and variable-cost analysis |
| Integrations/API | Institutions or partners will pay for interoperability | Specific partner demand, security review, stable API and support plan |

## Team growth triggers

Do not hire against speculative user forecasts. Use operational triggers:
- Customer success/implementation: onboarding backlog or support volume exceeds agreed response capacity.
- DevOps/SRE: deployment, reliability or capacity work repeatedly displaces product delivery or service objectives are missed.
- QA: release risk and regression workload exceed the engineering team's sustainable coverage.
- Business development: repeatable buyer profile, sales process and conversion evidence exist.
- Security/privacy: institutional requirements or risk profile require dedicated independent expertise.
- Additional product/backend/frontend engineers: a measured roadmap bottleneck and funded delivery plan exist.

For each role, record the trigger, employment type, fully loaded monthly cost, expected outcomes and review date.

## Quarterly operating review

At each quarter boundary:
1. Compare actual revenue and cash collection with forecast.
2. Review customer acquisition, activation, retention and renewal evidence.
3. Review cost to serve, cloud usage and third-party charges.
4. Reassess hiring and infrastructure thresholds.
5. Update conservative/base/growth forecasts.
6. Reprioritize roadmap against customer evidence and security obligations.
7. Decide whether the next funding milestone is justified.

## What must not be claimed prematurely

Do not claim a particular number of paying institutions, active users, revenue, retention, market share, regulatory approval, service availability, AI capability, partner integration or funding commitment unless supported by current evidence. Label aspirations as targets and pipeline as pipeline.
